Getting More Years From Your Water Heater in Church Hill

Most early failures trace back to maintenance that never happened, and the habits that prevent them are easy. Flush the tank every year to clear sediment, have the anode rod inspected every few years, and operate the relief valve annually so it doesn't seize. Keep the temperature near 120 degrees to reduce scald risk and slow scale buildup. For a tankless heater in a hard-water area, plan on descaling once a year so the heat exchanger stays clean.

How old is your water heater? The rating plate on the tank usually includes the manufacture date, and its age tells you a lot. Most tank heaters make it to 8 to 12 years, heat pump models 10 to 15, and well-maintained tankless units around 20. If your heater is past that point and is also showing rusty water, rumbling, or dampness under the tank, it's usually due for a planned replacement, and that's almost always cheaper and less stressful than a rushed emergency install once the tank fails.

The sooner you call about a hot water problem, the smaller the repair tends to be. A sediment layer that's just starting to rumble can often be fixed with a flush, yet the same sediment, neglected for a few years, can overheat the bottom of the tank and burn out the lower element on an electric model, forcing a replacement much sooner.
