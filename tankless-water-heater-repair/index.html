The majority of tankless breakdowns announce themselves the same way, with a blinking code on the display and a shower that turns cold. Behind that code is a system with very little margin for error. There is no storage tank to fall back on, so the heater has to detect flow, light a burner or power its elements, and raise the water temperature by fifty degrees or more in the few seconds it takes water to cross the heat exchanger. When scale narrows the passages, when the flow sensor stops reading, when the flame rod cannot confirm a flame, or when the vent or gas line cannot keep up, the unit protects itself by shutting down. That is the pattern we see week after week in Kingsport, TN.

Our approach to tankless repair is simple: measure first, replace second. When we arrive at your Kingsport home, we record the active code and the unit's fault history, then work through the systems behind it. Flow sensors are checked with water running to confirm they report demand correctly. Flame rods and igniters are tested electrically. Gas pressure is read at the inlet with the burner at maximum, because a line that holds steady at idle can sag badly under full load. The vent and air intake are inspected end to end, and the heat exchanger is judged by the temperature rise it achieves at a known flow. By the time we recommend a repair, we know it is the right one.

The good news is that most tankless problems are very fixable. A descaling flush clears most mineral buildup, sensors and igniters are replaceable parts, and venting faults usually come down to a blocked intake or a loose joint. We carry common parts for the major brands and service both gas and electric units, so the typical repair is done in one trip. We are also honest about the harder cases: a unit fed by a gas line too small for its full BTU rating will keep throwing codes until the line is upsized, and a heat exchanger damaged by scale may not be worth repairing on an older heater. And if your complaint is the cold-water sandwich, that brief burst of cold between two hot draws, we will tell you whether it is normal for your model or something we can reduce. Warning Signs to Watch For

Water heaters usually give plenty of warning before they fail, as long as you know what to look and listen for. These are the red flags our technicians see most often before a breakdown:

  • The display showing an error code instead of a temperature
  • New whistling, roaring, or rumbling from the unit
  • Lukewarm water whenever two fixtures run at the same time
  • Longer bursts of cold water between hot draws than you used to get
  • Hot water that fluctuates even though nobody touched the faucet
  • A clicking igniter with no flame, or a flame that lights and quickly drops out
  • Water dripping from the cabinet or the condensate line
  • Small draws, like a bathroom sink, that never trigger the heater

Don't Wait for a Total Failure

Tanks rarely fail all at once. They corrode from the inside for months, and the warning signs show up long before the floor gets wet. If you notice any of these signs, contact Kingsport Water Heater at (423) 398-3213 for a prompt inspection.

Why Hire a Professional for Tankless Water Heater Repair?

Start with what is at stake. A gas-fired tankless heater can burn nearly 200,000 BTU per hour at full fire, and its vent is the only thing carrying carbon monoxide out of the house; a joint that pulls loose or an intake that gets blocked changes that in a hurry. On the electric side, the loads are large, frequently more than 100 amps spread over several 240-volt circuits. Then there is the diagnostic puzzle. Error codes point to a system, not a part, so a flow sensor fault, a scaled heat exchanger, and a bad thermistor can look nearly identical on the display, and guessing gets expensive.

A trained technician with the right instruments gets to the answer faster. Ours read the fault history, measure flame signal and igniter resistance, test flow sensor output, and check gas pressure under full load with a manometer, which is the only reliable way to catch a gas line that holds pressure at idle but sags when the burner ramps up. Descaling is done with a circulating pump and the proper solution, followed by a thorough rinse. Just as important, you get an honest opinion: if the heat exchanger is damaged on an older unit, you will hear it from us before you spend money on a repair.

Water Heater Care Tips From Our Technicians

Routine maintenance is almost always cheaper and easier than an early replacement. Follow these tips from our technicians:

  • Have the unit descaled at least once a year, or more often if your water is hard
  • Keep the unit powered during freezing weather so its freeze protection can work
  • Consider a water softener or scale-reduction filter ahead of the heater if scale keeps returning
  • Get the supply line checked before adding gas appliances, which can starve a tankless unit
  • Rinse the small filter screen on the cold inlet every few months
  • Do not keep resetting a recurring code; note it and have it checked
  • Keep the vent termination and air intake clear of leaves, nests, snow, and lint
