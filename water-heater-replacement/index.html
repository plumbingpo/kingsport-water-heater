The end usually announces itself in one of a few ways: a slow leak from the bottom of the tank, rusty hot water, rumbling that grows louder every month, or a supply that runs out sooner each winter. When to Call for Water Heater Replacement

Most tanks announce trouble weeks or months ahead with rumbling noises, rusty water, or hot showers that keep getting shorter. Keep an eye out for the following:

  • Banging and popping from a tank packed with sediment
  • Rusty or brown water from the hot side only
  • A heater older than most tanks last, usually eight to twelve years
  • Rust flaking from the tank jacket or its connections
  • A household that has outgrown the tank's capacity
  • A second or third repair within a year or two
  • A slow leak or damp spot under the tank itself
  • Energy bills rising while hot water gets harder to come by

The Benefits of Professional Water Heater Replacement

Start with the venting. A gas water heater's exhaust contains carbon monoxide, and a vent that is undersized, poorly pitched, or loosely connected can let it spill back into your home. Add a gas connection that has to be leak-free, an electrical circuit that has to match the new heater's load, and a relief valve that has to carry the right rating with a discharge line that is never capped or undersized, and the details pile up quickly. Local code may also call for an expansion tank, drain pan, or seismic straps, and some areas want a permit. A move to tankless or a heat pump brings gas line sizing, new vent materials, circuits, and condensate lines into the job.

A professional replacement covers the whole installation, not just the tank. Sizing is done by first-hour rating or flow rate. Fuel, venting, and electrical capacity are verified, and whatever expansion tank, drain pan, or straps your home needs get added. Every gas and water joint is leak-tested, gas units get a draft and carbon monoxide check, and the old heater is hauled away. Water Heater Care Tips From Our Technicians

Most of the failures we see could have been delayed for years with basic upkeep. Here are our recommendations:

  • Write the install date on the tank so you know when to plan the next replacement
  • If you chose tankless, flush it annually to clear scale from the heat exchanger
  • Make sure an expansion tank is in place if your home has a closed plumbing system
  • A fresh anode rod at the right time is the cheapest way to extend tank life
  • Heat pump units need a clean filter and open airflow to run efficiently
  • Drain a few gallons every six months so sediment never gets a foothold
  • Set the thermostat around 120 degrees to prevent scalding and reduce wear
