Warning Signs to Watch For

A heater that is starting to struggle rarely hides it for long, and the clues are easy to spot once you know them. Keep an eye out for the following:

  • Dish or hand sink temperatures drifting below what your inspector expects
  • Rusty water or damp spots around the equipment
  • Unsteady delivery temperatures that suggest a failing mixing valve
  • Loud sediment noise from equipment that runs all day
  • Lukewarm water at the exact time you need the most
  • A recirculation loop that no longer seems to do its job
  • Higher utility bills that point to a heater losing efficiency
  • A heater old enough that a failure feels like a matter of time

Why This Job Calls for a Pro

Scaling up a water heater changes almost everything about it. Higher gas input means the supply line, meter, combustion air, and vent all have to be sized for the load. Heavy electric units can strain a panel that was never meant to carry them. Storage temperatures of 140 degrees or more help with sanitation but create scald risk, so properly sized mixing valves are essential. And a recirculation system only works when the pump, check valves, and balancing valves are all doing their part; if not, the far end of the building waits while the near end runs hot.

On top of the technical side come code and timing. Commercial installations usually involve permits, inspections, and sometimes additional requirements for larger vessels, and a job that fails inspection can leave a business without hot water for days. Our experienced technicians coordinate those steps before the old unit comes out, schedule work around your operating hours, and keep part of the system running where possible so you are never fully without hot water. How to Prevent Water Heater Problems

A few preventive steps can help you avoid a cold shower or a wet floor down the road. Follow these tips from our technicians:

  • Verify delivery temperatures at sinks and showers a few times a year
  • Log water temperatures at key fixtures so drift is caught early
  • Flush commercial tanks more often than residential ones, often every few months in heavy use or hard water
  • Check anodes on a schedule matched to your volume and water quality
  • Budget for replacement as equipment nears the end of its expected life
  • Leave space around commercial heaters for airflow and safe servicing
  • Listen for a failing recirculation pump and have check valves inspected
